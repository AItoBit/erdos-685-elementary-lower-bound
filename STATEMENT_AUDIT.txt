The complete proof concerns only the already stated textbook lower bound:
for natural 0<k<n, log(choose(n,k))/log(n) <= omega(choose(n,k)).
Omega here denotes the number of DISTINCT prime divisors, Mathlib's
ArithmeticFunction.cardDistinctFactors, not the multiplicity count.

The domain forces n>=2, hence log(n)>0 and choose(n,k)>0. Every full prime-power
block is <=n by Nat.pow_factorization_choose_le. Product reconstruction gives
choose(n,k)<=n^omega(choose(n,k)); log monotonicity and log_pow yield the claim.
There are no additional hypotheses or mathematical assumptions.

The original Erdos source prints a strict inequality, but n=2,k=1 gives equality.
The non-strict version agrees exactly with the existing Formal Conjectures
textbook statement. Source correspondence and the finite argument passed an
independent agent audit. No human expert review is claimed.

The two main asymptotic questions are separate and remain unproved here.
